Running pace anchors · body load

–

Body load for running follows your pace the way HR load follows heart rate. The two anchors are the pace you hold at your Easy limit and at your AP2 limit (≈ threshold). They're fitted from the last 30 days of steady running (grade-adjusted), or you can set your own.

HR load: points per minute shaped like a lactate curve, almost flat through Easy (1.0 at the Easy limit), 3.0 at the AP2 limit, then exponential. Body load adds 1 point per 10 m of descent. Strength/HIIT count 0.5 per minute (1 h ≈ 30 min easy running), yoga 0.1. Bike, ski and swim body load = HR load × 0.4 / 0.7 / 0.3. Saving zones recalculates all loads.

Neuromuscular

Short fast reps (sprints, strides, hill sprints) where heart rate can't keep up. A rep counts as neuromuscular when it's no longer than the rep length and faster than the minimum speed. Leave the speed empty to use "clearly faster than the rest of the session".
